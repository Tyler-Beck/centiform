14 March – 30 August 2026

One harp,
two hundred
and thirty years

In 1794 a cabinet-maker in Bristol built a pedal harp for a girl of eleven. It survived a shipwreck, a fire, and a century in a barn in Somerset. This exhibition shows one object and nothing else.

Walk slowly. Sit if you like. The harp is played on Sundays at 2 pm.

Room 2

Jonas Ashcombe

English, 1751–1819

Single-action pedal harp

1794

Sycamore, walnut, spruce soundboard, gilt gesso, brass action; 38 gut strings

Gift of the Pellow family, 1987

1987.114

Made for Anne Pellow on her eleventh birthday. The seven brass pedals raise each string by a semitone; Anne’s pencilled tuning notes are still inside the base.

Room 3

Jonas Ashcombe

Scroll and neck, detail

1794, regilded 1851

Carved sycamore, gesso, water gilding over red bole

1987.114 (detail)

The gilding you see is Victorian. Under the lamp in the corner, a scrape shows the first gold, redder and thinner, laid when the harp was new.

Hear it played

Sundays at 2 pm, Room 2. Rachel Onwu plays twenty minutes of music written between 1780 and 1810, on the harp and on a modern copy, so you can hear the difference. Free with admission. Seats for forty.
