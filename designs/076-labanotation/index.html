B Key: direction and level

The shape says where; the shading says how high. Press a sign to find it in the score.

C Reading this sheet

Start at the double line and read up. Time runs toward the top of the page; the length of a sign is the length of the movement. A sign two counts long is a slow step.

The centre line is the dancer’s spine. Signs just beside it carry the weight: these are the supports. The next columns out are leg gestures, then the torso, and outside the staff, the arms. Left is left, right is right, as if you stand behind the dancer.

A gap in both support columns is a jump. A leaning shape with slanted ends is a turn; the slant tells you which way. A small ring after an arm sign means hold it there until something else is written.

Varga counted in fours but danced across the bar. Kettering wrote exactly what she saw in the 1961 studio film, including the late weight shift in bar 6, which later dancers had smoothed out.
