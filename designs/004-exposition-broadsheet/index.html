The Gates Thrown Open.

Two Hundred Thousand Souls Pass the Turnstiles.

The Governor Presses the Golden Key and the Great Engine Wakes.

Flags of Thirty Nations.

At twelve minutes past noon yesterday, before a crowd that filled the Court of Industry from the lagoon to the colonnade, Governor Aldous Pryce laid his hand upon a key of Nevada gold, and the Lakeshore Industrial Exposition was declared open to the world.

The key closed a circuit to the Hall of Machinery, where the twin-cylinder engine of the Hollis Works, of fourteen hundred horse power, began at once to turn. Three hundred fountains rose together. The band of the Fourth Regiment struck up, and the guns of the revenue cutter Tamarack answered from the harbour.

Special to the Herald

Lost Children.

Eleven Restored to Parents by Sunset.

The bureau at the north gate reports that eleven children strayed from their families during the day. All but one were restored before the lamps were lit; the last, a boy of four answering to Tobias, was claimed at half past eight by a relieved father from Sheboygan.

Electric Light by Night.

Ninety Thousand Lamps Burn at Once.

The Lagoon Turned to a Sheet of Silver.

When the dusk fell the Exposition gave its second wonder. At a signal from the tower, the dynamos of the Western Electric Company were thrown in, and the white palaces stood out against the dark lake in lines of fire. Visitors who had seen the illumination at Paris declared this the finer.

The electrical board states that the lamps draw above three thousand horse power, and that no city in the Union burns so many in one square mile.

Riot of Wonder at the Wheel.

Cars Carry Sixty Apiece to a Height of 212 Feet.

The great Observation Wheel on the Midway, the work of Mr. Hiram Kettering of Pittsburgh, carried its first passengers at four o’clock. A ride of two turns costs fifty cents and takes twenty minutes; the view is said to take in four counties and, on a clear day, the smoke of Detroit.

Several ladies declined to leave the car at the summit and were carried round a third time at no charge. The engineer in charge, Mr. Pardee, assures the public that the axle, the largest piece of steel ever forged in one, would bear the wheel twice loaded.

Among the Exhibits.

What Our Reporter Found in Three Hours’ Walk.

A typewriting machine that prints in two colours; a loom that weaves a portrait of the President in silk; a cold-storage room kept at zero in which the ladies of the Dairy League have modelled a full-sized cow in butter.

The Canadian court shows a cheese of eleven tons, which it is proposed to cut on Dominion Day. A clock in the Swiss pavilion strikes the hours of forty cities at once, and a model of the harbour, with working locks, draws a crowd of boys that the guards cannot move.

Street Cars Overrun.

Company Adds Forty Trailers for To-Day.

The Merrivale City Railway carried, by its own count, ninety-one thousand fares on the Exposition line. Many rode on the steps and roofs. The superintendent promises cars every ninety seconds from six in the morning.

The Harbor Commission asks that visitors from the east shore use the new ferry, which lands at the Steamer Pier gate for five cents and takes eleven minutes.
