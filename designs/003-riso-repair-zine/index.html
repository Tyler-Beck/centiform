The Chainring Collective is a volunteer workshop in the back of the old Merritt Dairy garage, 418 Loom Street. We lend the stands, the tools and two spare hands. You do the fixing. Nobody here was born knowing how to true a wheel.

Punch card. Five Tuesday nights = one free chain, any speed. Cut along the line, bring it back, get it stamped at the counter by whoever is wearing the grease-proof apron.

Rule one. Every fix gets done by the owner of the bike. A volunteer will point, explain, and hold the wheel, but your hands turn the wrench. That is how the knowledge leaves the building with you.

2

Patch it in six

  1. 1

    Two levers

    Hook the first lever under the bead opposite the valve, clip it to a spoke. The second one runs around the rim.

  2. 2

    Find the hiss

    Pump the tube up fat. Pass it near your cheek or dunk it in the tub by the door. Mark the hole with chalk.

  3. 3

    Rough it up

    Sand a patch-sized circle around the hole. Shiny rubber will not hold glue; dull rubber will.

  4. 4

    Thin glue, wait

    One thin coat of vulcanising fluid. Wait until it goes from shiny to matt, about three minutes. Do not blow on it.

  5. 5

    Press hard

    Peel the foil, press the patch on from the centre out. Roll it with a tyre lever against the bench for a full minute.

  6. 6

    Seat and pump

    Tuck the tube in, push the valve up, roll the bead back with your thumbs. Pump to the number moulded on the sidewall.

The tool
wall

Every tool has a painted outline on the pegboard. If the outline is empty at 9 pm, nobody goes home. It has happened twice. Both times it was the 15 mm cone wrench, and both times it was in someone's back pocket.

Parts bin

Used parts come from donated bikes that are past saving. New parts are bought at cost. The jar by the till is honour system: pay the number, pay less, or pay more for the next person.
