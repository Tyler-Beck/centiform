Module 4: Reading the glass cockpit

Two screens replace eleven round dials. This module walks the left seat’s primary flight display and navigation display in a normal climb to 12,000 ft out of Harwick Regional.

Scroll to fly the lesson. The attitude indicator follows your progress.

Green: engaged, active modesCyan: values the crew has selectedMagenta: commands from the flight computerAmber: cautions

4.1 · 6 MIN

Attitude first

The blue-over-brown sphere is the most important thing on the flight deck. The white aircraft symbol is fixed; the horizon moves behind it. Right now we are in a gentle climbing turn.

The magenta cross is the flight director. Keep the aircraft symbol under the cross and you are flying what the computer asks.

4.2 · 4 MIN

Speed on the left

The speed tape moves, the box stays still. The magenta 250 above the tape is the target set by the flight management system: 250 knots below 10,000 ft.

A red and black checked bar at the top of the tape marks the maximum speed. You will see it in Module 7.

4.3 · 4 MIN

Altitude on the right

Current altitude sits in the black box. The selected altitude, 12000, shows at the top. The barometric setting below the tape is cyan because the pilot sets it.

4.4 · 5 MIN

Modes across the top

The flight mode annunciator tells you what the autopilot is doing, not what you asked it to do. Read it aloud after every change: SPD, LNAV, VNAV PTH, CMD.

Check: the FMA shows VNAV PTH. Who is controlling the rate of climb?

4.5 · 6 MIN

The route in magenta

On the navigation display the planned route is a magenta line from waypoint to waypoint. The active waypoint, KELSO, is magenta; the others are white.

The dashed cyan line is a modified route not yet executed. Nothing happens until you press EXEC.

4.6 · 3 MIN

Wind and ground speed

Top left: ground speed 312, true air speed 324, wind from 268° at 22 knots. The small arrow shows the wind relative to the aircraft. A 12 knot headwind component: why GS is lower than TAS.
