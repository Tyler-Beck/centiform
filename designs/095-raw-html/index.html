On keeping a web page for twenty-eight years

Posted Sunday 20 September 2026. About 4 minutes to read.

I made this page on a Tuesday in October 1998, in a text editor, on a computer that took four minutes to wake up. It had a grey background and a picture of a kestrel that took eleven seconds to load. The kestrel is gone. The grey went in 2001. Everything else you see is roughly as it was.

People ask why it looks like this. The honest answer is that I never found a reason to change it. The browser already knows how to make a heading big and a link blue. It knows to leave a line between paragraphs. It knows that a list should have bullets. I have simply let it do its job.

“A page that does nothing clever will still open in fifty years. A page that does everything clever will not open next spring.”

My neighbour Graham said that in 2004, when I asked him about frames. He was right about frames.

There are 214 essays here now. The oldest ones embarrass me, but I have not deleted them, because other pages link to them, and a link is a small promise. Twelve of the pages that once linked here are still alive. I check every New Year.

What has changed

  1. The address, once, in 2007.
  2. The typeface, never. It is whatever your browser likes.
  3. The width. It used to be 640 pixels. Now it is yours.
