Henry, by the grace of God King of England and of France, and Lord of Ireland,

Tto all to whom these present letters shall come, greeting. Know that of our special grace we have granted, and by this our charter confirmed, to our beloved and faithful men the Burgesses of Ashcombe and their successors for ever, the mill called Welbeck Mill upon the water of the Axe, with the leat, the weir, the eel-traps and the two acres of meadow lying between the weir and the king’s highway.

The mill still turns; it now grinds flour for the museum bakery.

To have and to hold of us and our heirs, rendering yearly to us at our Exchequer one pound of pepper at the feast of Saint Michael, and grinding without charge the corn of the poor of the said borough on every Saturday of the year.

And we will and firmly command that the said burgesses may hold a market in the said borough every Wednesday, and a fair every year lasting three days, namely on the eve, the day and the morrow of the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist, with all the liberties and free customs belonging to such a market and fair, unless the market or fair be to the harm of neighbouring markets and fairs.

The Wednesday market has not missed a week since, except in 1665.

Wherefore we will that the said burgesses and their successors shall have and hold all the aforesaid well and in peace, freely and quietly, wholly and honourably, as is aforesaid.

These being witnesses: the venerable father Henry, Archbishop of Canterbury;our dearest brother Thomas, Duke of Clarence; Richard, Earl of Warwick; and others.

Given by our hand at Westminster, the twelfth day of June, in the second year of our reign.

The common seal of the burgesses, in red wax on green silk cords, pressed 12 June 1414. Shown with the plica folded up, as the charter was delivered.
