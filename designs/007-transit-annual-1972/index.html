02 From the ChairmanLetter to the commissionersPages 4–5

A year of more riders, not higher fares.

Ridership rose every month but February, when the ice storm closed routes 4, 11 and 16 for six days.

Ten years ago this Authority was formed from three bankrupt private lines, a garage with a leaking roof, and 190 buses of which 60 would start in cold weather. In 1972 we ran 312 buses from four garages, and every one of them started.

The Crosstown Line, opened on May 1, is the first route in the region that does not run to or from the downtown terminal. It links the Oakmont mills, Bay Community College and the new county hospital. By December it was our fourth busiest route.

We did not raise the fare. The Board chose instead to seek the state operating grant, which paid 14 cents of every dollar we spent. We believe a thirty-cent ride is worth defending.

In 1973 we will begin work on the Harlan Avenue busway and order 60 more coaches. I thank our 1,406 employees, who made this year possible, and the riders who made it necessary.

Walter J. Okafor, Chairman
