Our main story

Writing Around a Missing Sound

Four authors, a lost glyph, and a jury that will not bring it back.

Our autumn copy is a lipogram. In it you will find no fifth symbol of our common ABC: not in a caption, not in a folio, not in any word of this story. A small counting script, up in our top bar, is watching for it, and it has found nothing.

Why do it? In 1969 a Paris author put out a full, long book this way, and it is still in print. What a constraint forbids, it also brings about: you find words you would not think of, and short, sharp syntax you would not risk.

Four authors took on our task: 200 words, no fifth symbol, a month to do it. Aud Brakstad sat down with a list of cooking words and lost half of it: no salt pan, only a pot; no fork, only a spoon.

Lucas Moraud built a whodunit about a lost cat, which by its final paragraph you will want found. Tomas Vilk, in Tartu, did it all in rhyming pairs and will not admit how long it took.
