200 m · Twilight zone

The last of the sun

Below two hundred metres there is not enough light to grow anything. Lanternfish rise past us every dusk, the largest migration on the planet, each fish trimmed with lights along its belly so that, from below, it has no shadow.

1 000 m · Midnight zone

Every light is a message

Here, nine animals in ten make their own light. A siphonophore colony, thirty metres long, drifts past the lander camera in blue and violet pulses; a dragonfish answers in red, a colour most fish cannot see.

The camera logs every flash. We count them the way astronomers count stars.

2 500 m · Bathypelagic

The anglerfish keeps its lamp

A female anglerfish, 11 cm, holds a lure of glowing bacteria over a mouth that can open wider than her body. Filmed for four minutes on dive 14 before she switched the light off and was gone.

4 000 m · Abyssal plain

The lander sits down

Mud as fine as flour, and sea cucumbers that walk across it on tube feet. The lander stays twelve days, then drops its ballast and floats home.
