Inv. L 214 · Building inscription from the aqueduct gate · limestone, H 84 cm, W 212 cm · letters 7.2 to 4.1 cm · AD 118–125

The Water Came Downhill

How the town of Vallis paid for its own aqueduct, and wanted everyone to know it.

The stone above hung over the arch where the new aqueduct entered the town. It is a dedication to the emperor, but read to the end: the last line, smaller and closer to the reader’s eye, says the town paid for it with its own money. That was the point.

TranslationTo the Emperor Caesar, son of the deified Trajan. The community of the Vallenses built the new aqueduct at its own expense.

The letters shrink line by line, from 7.2 centimetres at the top to 4.1 at the foot. A mason called the ordinator painted every letter on the stone first, with a brush, between ruled lines; the cutter followed the paint. You can still see two of his guide lines, scratched too deep, under the second row.

The red is modern. Traces of minium were found in the grooves in 1998, and the museum repainted them the next year, as a Roman visitor would have seen them. Press the button under the stone to show the ruling as the ordinator laid it out.

Move your pointer across the stone: the light follows it, as a torch held low against the surface does for the epigraphers in the store room.
